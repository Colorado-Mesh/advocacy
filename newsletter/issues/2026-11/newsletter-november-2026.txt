COLORADO MESH MONTHLY — NOVEMBER 2026
=====================================

Hello again, Colorado. This month the mesh looked outward. People offered high
ground in Fraser and Fort Morgan, and we still need an observer out toward
Sterling. Meanwhile the tools kept getting better: Mesh Client shipped several
releases, there is a MeshCore client for your wrist, and the emergency-comms
work around MECP kept moving. It is sorted by protocol, so jump to your corner
of the mesh.

GENERAL NEWS
------------

- Mesh Client kept shipping. After the big V6 release last issue, the client
  put out v6.6.0 on November 3, v6.7.0 and v6.7.1 on November 13 and 14, and
  v6.8.0 on November 16, and it is at v6.10.0 as of this writing. The
  community is also working on iOS and Android builds. Early layout tests are
  running, and a few members have started on the Android side. Release notes:
  https://github.com/Colorado-Mesh/mesh-client/releases

- MECP support keeps maturing. MECP (https://mecp.radio) is an
  emergency-messaging format for LoRa mesh that Mesh Client supports. This
  month MECP plain-text decoding started working in the Mesh Client TAK
  server, and MECP messages with location data now plot on a connected ATAK
  map and clear off it once they are resolved in the incidents tab.
  Contributors are also building shared test vectors so that different MECP
  clients read the same message the same way. The spec lives at
  https://github.com/mecp-org/MECP.

- Weekly Net reminder. A check-in is any message on the MeshCore Public
  channel between 12:00 a.m. and 11:59 p.m. on Thursday. It is not just the
  one-hour Discord event.

MESHTASTIC
----------

- A quieter month on the Meshtastic side. If you are setting up a new node,
  the wiki's Common Channels page lists the shared community channels:
  https://wiki.coloradomesh.org/index.php/Common_Channels

- Shared maps without cell service. Carroted Maps (formerly Flaresat) is an
  app that lets a group share one map over a paired MeshCore or Meshtastic
  radio, with no cell signal and no account. The November update added route
  sharing, alert zones, and a 24-hour replay. Radio and mesh features are
  free; the paid tier covers things like longer replay history and weather
  layers.

MESHCORE
--------

- WearMesh puts MeshCore on your wrist. budt W0RMT released a first test
  version of WearMesh, a MeshCore client for Wear OS smartwatches. The watch
  talks straight to your companion radio over Bluetooth, with no phone needed,
  for channel and direct messages. It needs a Wear OS 4+ watch (a Pixel Watch
  is the development platform), a companion radio on BLE firmware, and a
  computer with adb to sideload it. The author is looking for early testers
  and notes the app was built with AI assistance. Release and tester guide:
  https://github.com/Colorado-Mesh/wearmesh/releases/tag/v0.1.0

- FluxCore, a new MeshCore fork. beala announced FluxCore, an independent fork
  of MeshCore. One feature: instead of discarding packets that arrive damaged,
  it tries to recover them and logs them to a special "Damaged" channel, with
  unrecoverable bytes shown as "▒". A time-of-flight ranging feature that
  estimates the distance between two nodes is in progress. It is early work.
  Announcement: https://beala.substack.com/p/fluxcore-a-new-meshcore-fork

- Ideas from the Dutch MeshCore community. mplewis shared the repeater
  features in the latest Dutch MeshCore release: per-origin advert windows,
  path-prefix blocking, a dry-run mode, saved-airtime counters, rules for
  group text (block, throttle), and a configurable message-age limit. Worth a
  read if you run a busy repeater:
  https://github.com/Dutch-MeshCore/MeshCore/releases/tag/dmc-repeater-dev

- Good to know: MeshCore is built for reach, not secrecy. When someone asked
  whether MeshCore suits a privacy-focused group, cj gave a straight answer in
  #meshcore
  (https://discord.com/channels/1436156966648152271/1436175539135058082):
  group-text packets and their repeats can be seen, even when the channel and
  message contents are not known, and you can see that on the map in Mesh
  Client. For anything that absolutely has to stay private, cj pointed to
  Reticulum (if you need LoRa) or an encrypted messenger like Signal. Treat
  what you send on the public mesh as public.

- Small tip from jk: battery statistics are now available from the MeshCore
  CLI.

RETICULUM
---------

- Rettui 2.0 is out, and it is a one-way upgrade. Zeva's Reticulum text UI (a
  separate project from Mesh Client) released v2.0.0 on November 16. Version
  2.0 changes the storage backend to one that is not compatible with 1.x, so
  once you upgrade there is no going back. It also includes critical security
  patches, and Zeva recommends everyone upgrade. A fuller breakdown of the
  issues is planned once the release has been out a few weeks. Releases:
  https://github.com/zevaryx/rettui/releases

- More in Rettui since last issue. Version 1.10 added a Repos tab for
  browsing, cloning, and mirroring git repositories hosted on rngit nodes over
  Reticulum, plus nine more built-in games.

- A driver for the truSDX. James W5PL wrote a driver that makes the truSDX a
  usable radio for Reticulum:
  https://github.com/jwheeler188/W5PL-truSDX-Driver

- No RF needed to try Reticulum. The Colorado Mesh RRC hub is still the
  easiest on-ramp. If you have an internet connection, you can join and get a
  feel for it before buying a radio. Join in #reticulum
  (https://discord.com/channels/1436156966648152271/1455311030832992298).

GEAR & FIRMWARE
---------------

- Cold-weather battery warning. Lithium-ion cells should not be charged below
  freezing (32°F / 0°C). JohnnyW5KV looked for a battery management board for
  single-cell packs that cuts off charging in the cold and found that most do
  not. Multi-cell (3S or 4S) packs usually have temperature protection.
  Another option is a charger built around a thermistor, or a
  low-temperature-rated cell. His reassurance for small solar setups: the
  plating damage that cold charging causes is worst at high charge rates, and
  a small panel pushing an amp or two is low risk. If your solar node is
  heading into a Colorado winter, plan for this before the first hard freeze.

- Dual-band, 1 watt on both. beala sent the first messages through a custom
  WisBlock module with 915 MHz and 2.4 GHz radios, each at 1 watt.

- A neat 3D-printed case for the Heltec V4. Pete's design gives easy access to
  the hardware pins and has a slot for an SMA dongle. An RJ11 jack carries
  UART, a cheap way to chain other microcontroller boards.

- A bigger battery for the T-Deck. Half Live shared a custom T-Deck case with
  an 8000 mAh battery and an external GPS antenna.

- Enclosure tip. Zeva recommends PETG for its temperature resistance. For an
  attic install, PETG is plenty. JohnnyW5KV adds that if you put a repeater by
  a window, older windows are better for RF.

- A touchscreen for a home node. JohnnyW5KV suggested pairing an Elecrow
  Crowpanel Advance with an SX1262 radio module as a touchscreen front end for
  home mesh setups.

STORY OF THE MONTH: EAST AND UP
-------------------------------

Much of the conversation about the Colorado mesh has centered on the Front
Range. In November, the people who showed up were offering to extend it.

On the eastern plains, a newcomer who commutes between Golden and Fort Morgan
introduced themselves and offered to install and maintain a node on property
in Fort Morgan. They asked the right questions: are there existing nodes
around Hudson and Wiggins, and would Meshtastic or MeshCore be more useful out
there? Later in the month, another member asked whether MeshMapper could cover
the stretch from Fort Morgan to Sterling. The answer from nwithan8 was
practical: the area needs an observer before the map can fill in. There is a
to-do list in that exchange, and it is an open invitation.

In the mountains, slushbob switched from Meshtastic to MeshCore and brought up
a node west of Fraser, overlooking the valley. Another member started planning
a repeater in the same area and a link through Middle Park, with a friend near
Grand Lake. nightcrawler planned a trip to the property near Kremmling over
Thanksgiving week to get a repeater running again. Out west, the question of a
repeater on FlatTop came up in the Western Slope channel.

None of this needs a big budget. Each site is one person with a good view and
a willingness to tinker. A hill or a ridgeline that nobody has covered yet is
often what makes the difference between scattered coverage and a connected
region, so if you have access to high ground on the plains east of Denver or
in Middle Park, say so in #mesh-planning. Someone is probably looking for
exactly that spot.
